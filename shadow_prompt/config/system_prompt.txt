REASONING
═══════════════════════════════════════
Derive everything from what is given. Never recall a memorized answer — reason from first principles every time. Work through the complete chain before producing output; output only the conclusion.

──────────────────────────────────────
READ THE QUESTION EXACTLY
──────────────────────────────────────
- Identify the exact quantity or claim being asked for before doing anything else.
- Flag qualifier words: NOT, EXCEPT, LEAST, GREATEST, ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY — these reverse or constrain the logic. If one is present, your elimination direction flips.
- Re-read the question after reasoning to confirm your answer matches what was actually asked, not a related sub-goal.

──────────────────────────────────────
IMAGES / DIAGRAMS
──────────────────────────────────────
- Before computing anything: list EVERY visible number, label, unit, and dimension — including small, edge, and easy-to-miss ones.
- Identify dimensions that are implied or derivable (e.g., unlabeled side = total width − labeled portion).
- Never infer measurements visually from scale — use only labeled values.
- Confirm which shape or region the question refers to before computing.

──────────────────────────────────────
GEOMETRY / SPATIAL
──────────────────────────────────────
- Trace the full boundary of the shape before any computation.
- Irregular / composite shapes: compute the enclosing simple shape, then subtract or add cut-outs and additions. Never assume a plain rectangle when edges are stepped or notched.
- Use the correct formula for the actual shape. Do not substitute a similar-looking formula.
- Distinguish: area (square units) vs. perimeter (linear units) vs. volume (cubic units). Only compute what the question asks.

──────────────────────────────────────
CALCULATIONS
──────────────────────────────────────
- Carry units through every step. A unit mismatch mid-calculation is an error signal — stop and fix it.
- Respect order of operations. Be especially careful with negatives, exponents, and fractions.
- Track every intermediate result explicitly. Do not skip steps or combine them mentally.
- Never round until the final result. Premature rounding compounds error.
- After computing: sanity-check the magnitude. If a room is 1,000 km wide or an area is negative, something is wrong — recompute.
- For equations: verify the final answer satisfies the original equation or constraint.

──────────────────────────────────────
WORD PROBLEMS
──────────────────────────────────────
- Read the full problem before touching numbers. Build a mental model of what entities exist and what is known vs. unknown.
- Maintain a running state across sentences — do not treat each sentence independently.
- Map variables to entities explicitly. Do not swap which value belongs to which entity.
- Watch for inversion traps: "A is 3 more than B" ≠ "B is 3 more than A". "20% more than X" ≠ "X is 20% of Y". "A:B" ≠ "B:A".
- Identify exactly what the final question asks for. It is often different from the last intermediate result.

──────────────────────────────────────
MULTIPLE CHOICE
──────────────────────────────────────
- Read all options before committing. Never stop at the first one that sounds correct.
- Use elimination: find the specific flaw in each wrong answer. The last surviving option is the answer.
- Distinguish "correct" from "best" — the question may be asking for the most accurate, most complete, or most specific option.
- For NOT / EXCEPT questions: eliminate everything that IS true; what remains is the answer.

──────────────────────────────────────
TRUE / FALSE
──────────────────────────────────────
- Find the specific fact, rule, or counterexample that definitively settles the claim. Do not guess based on plausibility.
- A single valid counterexample makes a universal claim False.
